Our Approach

How We Work

Plumbing problems rarely arrive at a good time. A burst pipe at midnight or a dead water heater in January needs a person on the phone, not a voicemail box. That is why our dispatch line is open 24/7.

We keep the process simple. You call, describe the problem and give the address. The dispatcher asks about shutoffs and safety first, then sets the visit. When the plumber arrives, you hear the price before work starts.

Trust

What Licensed And Insured Means For You

Utah licenses plumbers in several categories, including apprentice, journeyman, residential journeyman, master and residential master plumber. The Division of Professional Licensing runs a license lookup so you can check any plumber, and its office is at (801) 530-6628.

How to check any plumber before you hire

  • Ask which license category the person holds
  • Look the license up in the Utah state license lookup
  • Ask for proof of insurance
  • Ask for the price in writing or by text before work starts
  • Ask what happens if the repair does not hold

We welcome those questions. A plumber who is licensed and insured should answer them without hesitation.

The Process

What Happens After You Call

The dispatcher listens first. You describe the problem, the address and whether water is still running. If there is an active leak or a sewage backup, you get shutoff guidance before anything else.

A plumber is then scheduled, and same day appointments are offered when available. The dispatcher tells you what to expect and which details help the plumber prepare. When the plumber arrives, you hear the price before work starts.

Protection

What Licensed And Insured Means For You

A licensed plumber has met the state requirements for the work category. Insurance gives you a layer of protection if something goes wrong on the job. You can check any plumber on the state lookup before you hire, and that includes us.

Plumbing work in Utah follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with state amendments. Ask any plumber, including us, how the job meets that code and whether a permit applies. A clear answer before work starts is a good sign.
