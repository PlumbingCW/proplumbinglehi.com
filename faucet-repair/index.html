Common Faults

Faucets Fail In Predictable Ways

Most faucet problems come from a small internal part. A cartridge wears out, a washer hardens or a seat pits, and a drip begins. Fixing the part is often cheaper than replacing the whole faucet.

Faucet typeCommon failureTypical repair
CartridgeDrip or hard to turnReplace the cartridge
CompressionDrip when closedReplace washers and seats
Ball or discLeak at the base or spoutReplace seals or the cartridge
Shower valveTemperature swings or leaks behind the wallReplace the cartridge or the valve
Outdoor spigotLeaks at the handle or freezesRepack or replace the spigot

If the fixture is old and parts are hard to find, we explain whether a new faucet is the smarter choice.

Under The Sink

Shut Off Valves That Will Not Shut

Many repairs fail on the first step, because the shutoff valve under the sink will not close. Old stop valves can seize or leak at the stem. We replace them so future repairs do not require shutting off the whole house.

If a supply line bursts and the valve will not turn, close the main shutoff and call. That is a leak emergency, not a faucet repair.

Behind The Wall

Shower And Tub Valves

Shower valves live inside walls, so a leak there may show up as a stain or a damp ceiling below. Temperature swings, a handle that will not stay put and water that will not shut off all point to a worn cartridge or valve. Replacing a cartridge is simpler than replacing a valve, and the plumber explains which it is.

If the problem looks like a pipe leak instead, our leak detection service can locate it before any wall is opened.

Hose Bibs

Outdoor Spigots And Winter

Outdoor spigots suffer in cold weather when a hose stays attached. Water freezes in the pipe behind the valve and splits it, and the damage shows up in spring when the faucet is turned on. Disconnect hoses before the first hard freeze, and replace a dripping spigot before it fails.

Lehi bars irrigation use after October 15, so that date is a good reminder to disconnect hoses and drain outdoor lines.

Diagnosis

Reading The Drip Before The Visit

Where the water shows up tells you what failed. A drip from the spout usually points to a worn cartridge, washer or valve seat. Water around the base of the handle points to a worn O ring or packing nut. Water under the sink may come from a supply line, a shutoff valve or the drain connection.

Weak flow from one faucet while the others run fine is often a clogged aerator. Unscrew the tip, rinse out the mineral grit and screw it back on. If the flow stays weak, the cause may be inside the faucet body or in the supply line.

Older faucets sometimes use parts that are no longer made. When a repair would cost close to the price of a new faucet, we say so before work starts. You approve the number, and you decide whether to repair or replace.
