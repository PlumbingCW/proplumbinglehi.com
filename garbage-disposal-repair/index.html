Safety First

Safe Steps Before You Call

Never put your hand in a disposal

  • Switch off the disposal and the breaker before touching anything
  • Use a wooden spoon handle or a tool to free a visible jam
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom after the jam clears
  • If it leaks, turn off the water under the sink and call
Emergency Response

Decision Guide

Repair Or Replace

Repair when

  • The unit hums but is jammed
  • A drain connection is loose
  • The unit is fairly new and sound
  • The reset button restores power

Replace when

  • The housing leaks
  • The motor is dead
  • The unit is old and keeps jamming
  • You want a stronger or quieter model
Installation

Installing A New Disposal

Replacement includes removing the old unit, cleaning the sink flange, mounting the new unit and connecting the drain and the dishwasher line. The plumber also checks the trap and the sink drain for buildup. The visit usually takes an hour or two.

Choose horsepower by use. A small household with light use does well with a standard unit, while a busy kitchen benefits from a larger motor. We recommend a size and you choose.

Often Overlooked

Dishwasher Connections

A leak under the sink can be the dishwasher drain hose or air gap rather than the disposal. We check the connections as part of the visit. If the whole kitchen drain is slow, a drain cleaning visit may be needed after the disposal work.

Troubleshooting

What The Hum Means

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. Switch it off at the wall and at the breaker, then look into the drain with a flashlight. Never put a hand inside, even when the power is off.

Most units have a hex socket on the underside where an Allen wrench can turn the plate back and forth by hand. Many also have a red reset button near the bottom that trips when the motor overheats. If a reset and a hand turn do not free it, the motor or the grinding parts may have failed.

A leak from the bottom of the unit usually means an internal seal has failed and the disposal needs replacing. A leak at the top flange or the drain connection can often be resealed. Run cold water while grinding, feed small amounts at a time, and keep grease, bones and fibrous peels out of the unit.
