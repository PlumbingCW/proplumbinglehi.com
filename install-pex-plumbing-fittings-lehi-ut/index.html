How To Install PEX Plumbing Fittings: A Lehi Homeowner Guide

PEX is forgiving, but the fitting must match the tubing and the tool must match the fitting. Here is how each type works.

By the Lehi Pro Plumbing team · Published November 6, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

To install a PEX fitting, cut the tubing square, slide on the ring or clamp, insert the fitting fully and compress the connection with the right tool. The four common systems are crimp, clamp, expansion and push to connect, and each uses its own tool and fittings. Mixing parts between systems is the most common cause of leaks.

PEX is popular because it bends around corners and needs fewer joints than copper. It is a good material for repairs and new work, and our plumbing installation service uses it for many projects.

Fitting Types

Four Ways To Connect PEX

SystemHow it sealsTool needed
Crimp ringA copper ring is squeezed around the tube and fittingCrimp tool and go no go gauge
Clamp ringA stainless clamp is cinched downCinch tool
ExpansionTubing is expanded then shrinks onto the fittingExpander tool
Push to connectA fitting grips the tube when pushed inNo special tool, but check approval

Pick one system for the whole project and use matching parts. Rings from one brand may not fit tools from another.

Get Ready

Tools And Supplies

Gather before you start

  • A PEX cutter that gives a square, clean cut
  • The tool that matches your fitting system
  • A gauge to check crimp rings if you use them
  • A deburring tool for the cut edge
  • Marking pen to show insertion depth
Process

The Steps For A Crimp Joint

1

Cut square

Cut the tube cleanly and remove any burrs.

2

Slide on the ring

Place the ring about a quarter inch from the end.

3

Insert the fitting

Push the fitting all the way in until the tube hits the shoulder.

4

Crimp and check

Squeeze the ring with the tool and verify it with the gauge.

Do not skip the gauge check. A ring that is slightly off can seep slowly and wet a wall for months before anyone notices.

What Goes Wrong

Common Mistakes

Cut not square

An angled cut can leave a gap at the fitting shoulder and leak.

Fitting not fully inserted

The tube must reach the shoulder or the ring grips only part of the barb.

Wrong tool

A clamp tool on a crimp ring will not seal.

Kinks and sun

A kinked tube is weak. PEX is also damaged by long sunlight exposure.

Test every joint under pressure before you close the wall. Leave the system under pressure for at least a few hours and check for drips.

Utah Winters

Freeze And Cold Weather

PEX tolerates freezing better than rigid copper, because it can expand slightly. That does not make it freeze proof. A frozen line can still burst or damage fittings. Insulate pipes in garages, exterior walls and crawlspaces.

If a pipe does freeze and split, see our burst pipe repair page for the shutoff steps and what to do while a plumber is on the way.

Good Practice

Tools And Habits That Prevent Leaks

Cut PEX with a proper tube cutter so the end is square. A ragged cut keeps the fitting from sealing. Slide the ring on before you insert the fitting, and check the insertion depth against the maker marks.

Use the tool that matches the system. Crimp rings need a crimp tool and a go no go gauge, while expansion systems use a different expander. Mixing parts from different systems is a common cause of leaks.

Support horizontal runs at regular intervals so the line does not sag, and keep PEX out of direct sunlight because ultraviolet light damages it. Pressure test the system before you close the walls.

Know Your Limits

When To Call A Plumber Instead

PEX is friendly to learn, but a few jobs deserve a professional. Connecting to a water heater, moving a main line or tying into old galvanized pipe all carry risks that show up later as leaks. Mixed metals can also corrode where they meet, and the right transition fitting depends on what is already in the wall.

Call us if you are unsure about the connection type or see green crust on old copper. We can also pressure test your finished work before you close the wall.
