How To Install A Toilet Shut Off Valve In Lehi

A working shutoff valve behind the toilet turns an emergency into a minor job. Here is how to install one.

By the Lehi Pro Plumbing team · Published September 22, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

To install a toilet shut off valve, turn off the water to the house, flush the toilet to empty the tank, disconnect the old supply line and valve, fit the new valve onto the pipe and reconnect the supply line. The part that trips people is the pipe type, because the valve has to match what comes out of the wall. Copper, plastic and flexible stub outs all use different fittings.

If the valve is old and will not turn, replacing it is one of the best small plumbing upgrades you can make. If you would rather hand the job over, our toilet repair service includes new shutoff valves.

Match The Fitting

Identify The Pipe Before You Buy The Valve

Pipe coming from the wallTypical valve connectionNotes
Copper stub outCompression valveSlides over the pipe and tightens with a nut and sleeve
Plastic stub outPush or compression styleCheck that the valve is rated for the pipe type
Threaded iron or brassThreaded valveNeeds thread sealant and care to avoid cracking
Soldered copperSoldered valveNeeds a torch and is better left to a plumber

Take the old valve to the store if you are unsure. The size printed on the pipe or valve helps, and most toilet supply lines use a three eighths inch fitting at the valve end.

Before You Start

Tools And Parts

Gather these first

  • A new quarter turn shutoff valve that matches the pipe
  • A new braided supply line
  • Adjustable wrenches or a basin wrench
  • A small bucket and a towel
  • Thread sealant only if the valve is threaded
Process

The Steps

1

Shut off the water

Close the main shutoff and open a faucet to relieve pressure.

2

Empty the tank

Flush the toilet and sponge out what remains.

3

Remove the old valve

Disconnect the supply line, then loosen the compression nut and slide the valve off.

4

Install the new one

Slide on the nut and sleeve, seat the valve and tighten until snug.

Reconnect the supply line, turn the water back on slowly and check for drips at the valve and at the line. Tighten a quarter turn at a time and stop when the drip stops. Overtightening can crack the sleeve and cause a new leak.

Problem

When The Main Valve Will Not Close

The first step needs a working main shutoff. If the house main will not close or does not hold, do not start the job. A failed main valve is a repair of its own and may point to an older line that needs attention.

Lehi owns the water meter and the valve at the curb. Do not operate the city valve yourself. Call Lehi Water and Sewer at (385) 201-1700 in office hours or 801-836-1045 after hours if you need the city to shut water off.

Local Note

Water Basics For Lehi Homes

The meter marks the handoff between city and owner. A leak before the meter belongs to the city, while the pipe after the meter belongs to you. If a valve behind a toilet fails and water is spraying, close the house main first, then call us.

Homes built before 1986 are more likely to have older fittings and, according to Lehi City, a higher chance of lead pipe or solder. If your valves are decades old, use the new valve as a prompt to look at the whole run.

Know Your Limits

When To Call A Plumber

Call a plumber if the pipe is soldered, if the wall is wet around the stub out, or if the pipe moves when you turn the valve. A pipe that is not secured inside the wall can break when you tighten a fitting. Stub out repairs like this are routine work for our crews in Lehi and in American Fork.

If you are chasing a leak that you cannot find, our leak detection service can locate it before any wall is opened. The service areas page lists the towns we serve.

Parts

Choosing The Valve And The Supply Line

Shutoff valves come in compression, push fit and soldered styles. Compression valves suit most do it yourself swaps on copper pipe because they need no torch. Quarter turn valves are easier to use than old multi turn valves that seize from lack of use.

Replace the braided supply line when you replace the valve. Old lines can bulge or crack at the ends, and a failed line is a common cause of an indoor flood. Tighten the nut by hand and then snug it with a wrench, since overtightening can damage the washer.

After the swap, open the valve slowly and check every joint with a dry paper towel. Leave the towel in place for a few minutes and look again. Close and open the valve once a year so it does not freeze in place.
