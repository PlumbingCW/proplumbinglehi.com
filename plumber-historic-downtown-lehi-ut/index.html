The Neighborhood

About Historic Downtown Lehi

The Lehi Main Street Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 10, 1998. It covers about 5 acres roughly along Main Street between 200 West and Center Street, with 19 contributing buildings dated 1891 to 1934. Lehi City Hall, the library and the Lehi Banner Office are among them.

Homes and small businesses surround that district. The city has a downtown revitalization plan, and the area mixes shops, offices and houses of many ages. We do not assume anything about a building from its address alone.

Practical Notes

Plumbing Notes For Older Buildings

This section is general guidance. A building that is old may have been updated many times, and a building that looks new may hide old work. We inspect the actual pipes before recommending anything, and we do not guess materials from age.

Before a repipe in any older building, ask about lead testing. The city links construction before 1986 to a higher chance of lead in house plumbing. Our repiping page explains how a replacement plan usually works.

Older sewer lines can have roots and offsets. A camera inspection shows the real condition of the line before you commit to digging. Our sewer line repair page explains the options.

Local Note

Historic District Rules

If your building is inside the Main Street Historic District, exterior changes may be reviewed. Plumbing work inside the walls usually is not, but check with Lehi City before you plan a visible change. We do not give legal advice about historic rules.

For shops, restroom and floor drain calls can run after hours. See commercial plumbing for business service.

Before You Call About A Downtown Property

Have the street address, the approximate age of the building if you know it, and a short description ready. Say whether the problem is in a home, a shop or an upstairs unit. That helps the dispatcher send the right tools the first time.

Tell us if a previous plumber worked on the line, and share any photos of the leak or the clog. If a landlord or property manager owns the building, check who approves repair costs before work starts. We give a clear price before work starts, so you can pass it along.
