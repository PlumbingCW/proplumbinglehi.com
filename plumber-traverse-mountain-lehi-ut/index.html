The Neighborhood

About Traverse Mountain

Traverse Mountain is a large master planned community on the hillside at the north end of Lehi. Listing data describes it as covering more than 2,500 acres with a plan for more than 5,000 homes. Its first homes date to the early 2000s and later phases are still being finished.

The community mixes single family homes and townhomes, with larger custom homes on the upper streets. It is split into named neighborhoods such as Ridgeview, Canyon Point, Fox Canyon and Heather Moor. The Outlets at Traverse Mountain sit at the bottom of the hill.

Practical Notes

Plumbing Questions That Come With A Hillside

The facts above are local. The advice in this section is general plumbing guidance and applies to any sloped lot. We do not know the pipe material or condition of your home from the neighborhood name, and we will not guess before looking.

Water pressure can differ between a house low on a slope and one near the top. A pressure gauge on an outdoor spigot tells you what the house sees. If the reading is high, a failing pressure regulator is a common and inexpensive thing to check before blaming the pipes.

Water also follows slopes. After heavy rain or snow melt, a wet basement wall or a sump pit that keeps filling points at drainage first. Sort out whether the water is coming in from outside or leaking from a supply line, because the fixes are different.

Local Note

Who Handles What In An HOA Community

Many Traverse Mountain homes belong to a homeowners association. Townhomes may share walls, roofs or drain lines with a neighbor, and the HOA documents decide who pays for shared parts. Read those documents before you call anyone for a leak inside a wall.

Lines that serve only your unit are generally yours to fix. Shared stacks and mains are often the association responsibility. We will tell you which one we are looking at, and you can confirm the rule with your management company.
