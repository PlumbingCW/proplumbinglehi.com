How To Replace A Water Heater Dip Tube In Lehi

If your hot water runs lukewarm and the tank seems fine, the cold water inlet tube inside may have failed.

By the Lehi Pro Plumbing team · Published September 29, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

A dip tube is a plastic pipe inside the tank that sends cold water to the bottom so the burner or element can heat it. When the tube cracks or breaks, cold water mixes with hot at the top. The signs are lukewarm water, hot water that runs out fast and small plastic bits in aerators and filters.

Replacing a dip tube is a repair many handy people can do, as long as the water and gas or power are off. If the tank is old, replacement may make more sense, and our water heater replacement page explains how to decide.

Symptoms

Signs A Dip Tube Has Failed

Common clues

  • Hot water turns lukewarm after a short time
  • Tiny white or gray plastic bits in faucet screens
  • The water heater seems to run all the time
  • The tank feels hot at the top but the water at the tap is only warm
  • The tank is not leaking and the thermostat looks fine

Older plastic tubes from certain years were known to break down. Newer tubes use better plastic, but heat and age still take a toll.

Quick Checks

Rule Out Simpler Problems First

CheckWhat it tells you
Thermostat or gas control settingToo low a setting gives lukewarm water
Breaker or pilot lightNo power or flame gives cold water
Heating element on an electric unitA bad lower element gives lukewarm water
Sediment in the tankRumbling and reduced capacity
Plastic bits in aeratorsPoints strongly to a dip tube

Unscrew a faucet aerator and look for small pieces. If you find plastic bits, the dip tube is a strong suspect.

Process

The Steps To Replace It

1

Shut down

Turn off power or gas and close the cold water inlet.

2

Drain a few gallons

Open a hot tap and drain enough to lower the water level below the inlet.

3

Remove the cold nipple

Disconnect the cold line and unscrew the inlet fitting. The dip tube sits inside it.

4

Install the new tube

Slide in the new tube, reconnect the line and fill the tank before restoring power or gas.

Open a hot tap while the tank fills so air can escape. Check the inlet fitting for leaks, then restore power or gas. Run a hot tap for a minute to flush out plastic bits so they do not clog fixtures.

Know When To Stop

When Replacing The Tube Is Not Enough

If the tank is more than ten years old, rusty at the base or leaking from the body, a new tube will not solve the bigger problem. A dip tube repair makes sense on a tank that is otherwise sound. If the tank is failing, put the money toward a new unit.

Gas units add risk. If you are unsure about handling the gas control, shut off the gas, leave the repair and call us. See water heater repair for how we diagnose tanks.

Local Note

Scale, Water And Your Tank

A tank collects sediment that reflects what is in your water over the years. The city reports we reviewed give no hardness figure, so we do not guess at yours and suggest a simple tap test.

If your fixtures show white crust, test your tap. Scale can build up in a tank and on an element, and a flush may help a tank that is otherwise sound.

Maintenance

Preventing The Next Failure

Drain a few gallons from the tank drain once a year to move sediment out. Check the anode rod on a schedule if the manufacturer calls for it. Keep the temperature setting reasonable, because very high settings stress parts and raise scald risk.

After The Repair

Checking The Result After The Swap

After the new dip tube is in, refill the tank completely before turning on power or lighting the burner. Open a hot tap somewhere in the house until the water runs steadily, which pushes air out of the lines. Then check the connections at the top of the tank for drips.

Give the heater a few hours to reach temperature before judging the fix. If the water is still lukewarm, the cause may be a failed thermostat, a heating element, a burner problem or heavy sediment. The tube is only one possible cause.

Flush a few gallons from the drain valve once a year to clear sediment. A tank that has never been flushed may have a valve that is stuck. Call us if it will not open, because forcing it can cause a leak.

Rule It Out

Other Causes Of Lukewarm Water

Lukewarm water also comes from a thermostat set too low, an undersized heater or crossed connections at a mixing valve or single handle faucet. Check the thermostat setting and try another faucet first. That tells you whether the problem is the heater or one fixture.
