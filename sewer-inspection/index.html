Good Reasons

Who Should Get A Sewer Inspection

Home buyers

A line under a house you may buy can hide cracks and root masses. A camera visit before closing helps you plan or negotiate.

Repeat clog owners

If a main line clogs more than once a year, a camera shows whether the cause is grease, roots or damage.

Older home owners

Houses with original lines may have clay, cast iron or older pipe that has aged unevenly.

Before a remodel

Adding a bathroom or basement fixture adds load. A camera confirms the main can handle it.

Process

What Happens During The Visit

1

Find the access

We locate a cleanout or pull a fixture such as a toilet if no cleanout exists.

2

Run the camera

The camera is fed down the line while you watch the screen.

3

Mark and measure

Problems are measured from the access point.

4

Review

We go over findings and what they mean for your plan.

The visit does not clear blockages. If the line is blocked, we clean it first, then run the camera. See sewer line cleaning for that service.

What We Inspect

Septic Systems And City Sewer

Camera inspections work on house lines connected to city sewer and on lines leading to a septic tank. Parts of Lehi use septic, and the tank and drain field need their own inspection by a septic specialist. We will say which parts of the system we can see.

Wastewater from city connected homes flows to the Timpanogos Special Service District, which serves Lehi and several nearby cities. The camera stops at the main or the property line, and city questions go to Lehi Water and Sewer at (385) 201-1700.

Using The Footage

Reading The Results Before You Spend

A camera result is a decision tool. A line in good shape means you can relax. A line with minor roots may need only a cleaning, while a collapsed section changes the numbers on a house purchase. Ask the plumber for the footage and notes so you can show them to your agent or insurer.

We do not oversell repairs. If the line looks sound, we say so and you pay for the inspection only.
