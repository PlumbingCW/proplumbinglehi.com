Know The Signs

Signs The Line Needs Repair, Not Just Cleaning

A cleaned line that clogs again within weeks is the classic sign. The cable opens a path, but a belly, a crack or a root intrusion catches debris again. Gurgling toilets, sewer smells in the yard and a wet patch over the line point the same way.

Warning signs of a damaged sewer line

  • Repeated main line backups after cleaning
  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once
  • Sewer odor near the cleanout or yard
  • A soggy or sunken strip of lawn along the line
  • Cracks or settling near the house foundation
Confirm First

Why We Run A Camera Before Any Dig

Guessing at a sewer problem is expensive. A camera on a cable shows the pipe material, the crack, the offset joint or the root mass. It also measures the distance from the cleanout so the repair can be planned for the right spot.

If you only need a look, our sewer inspection service covers that step on its own. When a repair is needed, the footage becomes the scope.

Responsibility

Who Owns Which Piece Of The Line

The city owns the sewer main, and your house line connects to it. Wastewater flows to the Timpanogos Special Service District treatment system, which serves Lehi and several nearby cities. Ask Lehi Water and Sewer at (385) 201-1700 where the city main begins if you need to know who owns a failure.

Before any digging, call 811 so buried utilities are marked. We handle that step as part of the plan, and we explain what restoration of the yard or driveway will look like before work starts.

Different System

Septic Systems In Parts Of Lehi

Some Lehi properties still use septic. A backup there can mean a full tank, a failed baffle or a drain field problem, and none of those are fixed by a sewer line repair. We will ask about your system on the call so you do not pay for the wrong visit.
