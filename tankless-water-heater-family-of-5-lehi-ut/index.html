What Size Tankless Water Heater Does A Family Of 5 Need In Lehi?

Family size matters less than how many fixtures run at once. Here is how to size a tankless unit for a busy house.

By the Lehi Pro Plumbing team · Published November 10, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

A family of five usually needs a whole house tankless unit rated around the high single digits to low teens in gallons per minute at winter temperature rise, or two linked units. The number comes from how many fixtures run at the same time, not from headcount. A busy morning with two showers, a dishwasher and a washer sets the real demand.

This guide shows how to count that demand and how winter water temperature changes the size. For a walk through of your home, see our tankless water heater service.

Peak Flow

Count The Fixtures That Run Together

FixtureTypical flow when running
ShowerAbout two gallons per minute with a standard head
Bathroom sinkAbout one gallon per minute
Kitchen sinkAbout one to two gallons per minute
DishwasherAround one gallon per minute at the hot tap
Clothes washerAround two gallons per minute at the hot tap

Add up what could run together at peak. Two showers, a washer and the kitchen sink can reach seven or eight gallons per minute. Those numbers are typical, so check your own fixtures.

Why Winter Counts

Winter Temperature Rise Changes The Size

A tankless heater is rated by how much it can raise the temperature at a given flow. Colder incoming water means a bigger rise is needed to reach the same hot temperature. A unit that handles your peak flow in July may fall short in January.

Always size from the coldest month. Ask the manufacturer for the flow rate at the rise you need, and be wary of marketing numbers based on mild water.

Sizing checklist

  • The number of fixtures that can run at once
  • The target hot water temperature at the tap
  • The coldest incoming water temperature
  • The unit flow rating at that temperature rise
  • The gas input rating and line capacity
Design Choice

One Large Unit Or Two Linked Units

One large unit

  • Simpler installation
  • Lower purchase cost
  • One thing to service
  • A single failure leaves no hot water

Two linked units

  • Higher peak capacity
  • One can keep running if the other fails
  • Needs more wall space and gas supply
  • Higher purchase and install cost

For many families of five, a single high flow unit is enough. If your layout has a distant bathroom or you have a large soaking tub, two units may fit better.

Gas Line

Gas Supply Is Often The Limit

Big tankless units draw a lot of gas. The existing line that fed a tank may be too small, and the meter may need to supply the extra load. Ask your gas utility whether the meter and line can handle the added demand.

A plumber measures the pipe run and checks line size before you commit. Venting is the other common limit, because large units need a larger vent path.

Care

Maintenance For A Busy Household

Heavy use means more heat cycles and more scale if the water is hard. Many manufacturers call for a flush on a regular schedule. Test your tap for hardness so you know whether a softener belongs in the plan, and read our water treatment page.

Tankless sizing visits cover Lehi and American Fork. For other addresses, check the service area page.

Sizing

Sizing By Fixtures, Not By Headcount

Flow rate in gallons per minute is the number that matters. Add up the fixtures likely to run at the same time, such as two showers and a dishwasher. Compare that total with the unit rating at your incoming water temperature.

A larger family does not always need a larger unit if showers are spread across the morning. But two teenagers showering while the laundry runs can strain a unit that looked big enough on paper. Think about your busiest hour, not your average one.

Gas lines and vent sizes often need upgrading for a whole house unit. Ask for those costs in the quote so the total is clear.

Cost

Questions About Cost And Install

A tankless unit costs more up front than a tank and can cut standby heat loss. Savings depend on how you use hot water, so treat any maker estimate as approximate. We give a clear price before work starts so you can compare.

Descaling matters too. Mineral buildup reduces performance, and many makers recommend periodic flushing. Ask how often your unit needs it and whether the install includes service valves for flushing.
