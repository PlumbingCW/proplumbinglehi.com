The Appeal

Why Homeowners Ask About Tankless

A tankless unit heats water only when you open a tap, so there is no tank to run out. It also hangs on a wall, which frees up floor space in a garage or utility room. Those are the reasons we hear most on the phone.

The trade is installation complexity. Most tankless heaters need a larger gas line, special venting and a water supply that is clean enough to protect the heat exchanger. Getting that right is what the first visit is for.

Side By Side

Tank Versus Tankless

Standard tank

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Simple installation with existing lines
  • Limited hot water until it recovers
  • Larger footprint in the utility space

Tankless

  • Steady hot water for back to back use
  • Wall mounted and compact
  • Needs gas line and venting review
  • Needs yearly flushing to stay efficient
Flow Rate

Sizing Comes Before Anything Else

A tankless unit is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat at a given temperature rise. Cold incoming water makes the rise larger, so a unit that works fine in July may struggle in January. We size for winter conditions, not summer.

Maintenance

Flushing And Descaling

Calls about tankless flushing are among the most common we receive. Minerals can coat the inside of the heat exchanger, and scale cuts both flow and efficiency. Many manufacturers call for a vinegar flush or a descaling cycle on a regular schedule.

Because Lehi City does not publish a hardness number in the reports we reviewed, we suggest testing your own tap. A simple test kit tells you whether scale is likely, and the result shapes how often to flush and whether a softener makes sense. See our softener and filtration page for the next step.

Install Details

Gas And Venting In Lehi Homes

Many tankless units need a gas line larger than the one that served a tank. The plumber measures the run and checks capacity before quoting. Venting may need stainless or plastic pipe through a wall or roof, and the path matters as much as the unit.

Utah amendments to the plumbing code include water heater rules, and Lehi Building and Inspections at (385) 201-1035 can tell you what the city needs for your job. If you smell gas at any time, leave the house, then call 911 and the gas utility.
