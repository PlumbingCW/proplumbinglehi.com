Most of these are repairs, not new toilets. If the toilet is old, cracked or constantly failing, replacement can cost less over time.

Try This First

Quick Checks Before You Call

Safe steps you can try

  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet if water is running or leaking
  • Lift the tank lid and check that the flapper seals on the bottom
  • Use a flange plunger for a clog and avoid chemical cleaners
  • Do not flush again if the bowl is full or rising

If water keeps rising or the supply valve will not turn, call right away. A valve that will not close is a repair of its own.

Emergency Response

Toilet Overflowing Late At Night

An overflowing toilet can soak a floor in minutes. Stop the flush, close the supply valve behind the toilet and put towels at the base. If the water is rising, call the 24/7 line and describe what is happening.

When other fixtures also gurgle or back up, the problem is deeper than the toilet. That is a job for sewer line cleaning, not a toilet repair.

Decision Guide

Repair Or Replace

Repair makes sense when

  • The toilet is in good condition
  • Parts like the flapper or fill valve are failing
  • The base seal is the only fault
  • The model is still available for parts

Replace makes sense when

  • The porcelain is cracked
  • The flush is weak and parts are discontinued
  • You want a more efficient model
  • The toilet is repaired repeatedly
Installation

Installing A New Toilet

New toilet installs include setting the flange, a new wax ring, new supply line and a shutoff valve if the old one is worn. We also check the floor for rot, because a soft floor is a hidden source of rocking. The visit usually takes a couple of hours.

Small Parts, Big Leaks

Supply Valves And Fill Valves

A shutoff valve behind the toilet that seizes can turn a small repair into a house wide shutdown. We replace worn supply valves and fill valves as part of the visit, since parts that are decades old tend to fail together.

Home Checks

Quick Checks Before You Call

To test for a silent leak, add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. A worn flapper is a common cause of a running toilet and a rising water bill.

Check the chain too. If it is too tight, the flapper cannot seal, and if it is too loose, the flush can be weak. The fill valve is another common failure when the tank keeps refilling.
