Planning

Start With The Space, Not The Unit

A good install begins with where the heater will sit. The room decides which fuel you can use, how the flue or exhaust runs and how a leak drains. Picking a model first often forces expensive changes later.

Garage or basement utility room

Most Lehi homes put the heater here. Floor drains, venting paths and gas lines are usually close by.

Closet or finished space

A leak here can reach flooring and drywall fast, so a drain pan and a safe discharge path are essential.

New construction

We coordinate with the builder so rough in, gas and venting match the model before walls close.

Relocation or upgrade

Moving a unit changes pipe runs and venting, so plan extra time and a written scope.

Scope

What A Complete Install Includes

Items we confirm on every install

  • Strapping in the upper and lower thirds of the tank where Utah rules call for it
  • A drain pan and pan drain line where a leak could damage finished space
  • A relief valve with a safe discharge pipe
  • An expansion tank on closed systems when needed
  • Shutoff valves at the inlet and gas supply
  • A final test with water running and the unit firing

Each item protects something specific. Strapping keeps the tank upright if the ground moves, a pan catches slow leaks, and the expansion tank protects fittings from pressure swings. We list each one in the scope so nothing is a surprise.

Local Rules

Permits And Inspections In Lehi

Lehi Building and Inspections handles permits through an online portal and schedules inspections by phone with 24 hours notice. The office is at 153 N 100 E and the number is (385) 201-1035. Permit requirements depend on the job, so we confirm what applies before the visit and tell you what to expect.

Utah also adds statewide amendments to the plumbing code that address water heater strapping and pan drains. We install to those rules and explain what was done when the job ends.
