Decision

Is It Time To Replace?

A water heater does not usually die without warning. Rust in the water, moisture around the base and a tank that has been running more than a decade are all signals. If the unit leaks from the tank itself, repair is no longer on the table.

Replace rather than repair when

  • Water leaks from the bottom or sides of the tank
  • The unit needs repeated repairs in a short time
  • The tank is rusting at the base or fittings
  • The unit cannot keep up with a larger household

If none of these apply, a water heater repair visit may save you the cost of a new unit. The plumber will tell you which side of the line your tank sits on.

Process

What Replacement Day Looks Like

1

Shut down

We turn off water, power or gas and drain the old tank.

2

Remove

The old tank and its fittings are disconnected and taken out.

3

Install

The new unit is set on a drain pan with proper strapping, venting and connections.

4

Test

We fill, purge air, check for leaks and confirm hot water at a tap.

Most tank swaps take a few hours. Tankless conversions and relocations take longer because they involve gas, venting and sometimes electrical work.

Local Code

Utah Install Rules Worth Knowing

Utah amendments to the plumbing code call for strapping at the upper and lower thirds of the tank. They also set rules for drain pans and pan drain lines where a leak could reach finished space. Permit requirements for replacements vary by city, so ask Lehi Building and Inspections at (385) 201-1035.

Where the tank sits in a closet or finished area, a drain pan and a safe discharge path matter more than the price of the unit. A small leak with no pan can ruin a floor before anyone notices.
