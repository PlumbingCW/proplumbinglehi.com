Whole House Plumbing Replacement: A Planning Guide For Lehi

Replacing all the plumbing is a big decision. Here is when it pays off, how the work goes and how to plan.

By the Lehi Pro Plumbing team · Published October 20, 2026

Talk to a plumber
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

A whole house plumbing replacement makes sense when the supply pipes are failing across the system, not just at one spot. Rusty water, falling pressure and a string of leaks in different rooms are the usual triggers. The project replaces supply lines throughout the house, and sometimes drains too.

Most replacements take a few days and involve opening walls. Planning the work before the first cut saves money. If you want to compare options, our repiping page lays out partial and whole house choices.

Warning Signs

Signs You Are Past Repairs

Clues that point to full replacement

  • Pinhole leaks in more than one room
  • Brown or orange water at taps
  • Galvanized or polybutylene pipe still in the walls
  • Pressure that has dropped over several years
  • Repairs that keep coming back in different places

A single pinhole leak does not mean the whole system is done. A pattern across rooms does. Ask a plumber to look at the pipe in the basement or crawlspace and compare repair costs against replacement.

Scope Choice

Supply Lines Versus Drains

SystemWhat it carriesWhen it is replaced
Supply linesPressurized clean water to fixturesMost common replacement, for corrosion and leaks
Drain linesWaste water by gravityOnly when cracked, collapsed or failing
Water service lineWater from the meter to the houseWhen buried line fails, see the service line page
Gas linesFuel to appliancesSeparate scope with its own safety rules

Many people say whole house plumbing when they mean the supply lines. Be clear with a contractor about which systems are in scope.

Materials

Pipe Choices

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer joints
  • Good freeze tolerance for its class
  • Quick to install in walls
  • Needs protection from sunlight

Copper

  • Long track record
  • Rigid and strong
  • More labor and fittings
  • Some water chemistry can cause pinholes

Either material is acceptable when installed correctly. The right pick depends on budget, the layout and your preference.

Sequence

How The Work Usually Goes

1

Plan and permit

A walk through, a written scope and any needed permit.

2

Open access

Small openings in walls, ceilings or floors to run new lines.

3

Install and test

New lines go in, fixtures reconnect and pressure tests confirm the work.

4

Patch and inspect

Openings are patched or left for a drywall contractor, and inspections are scheduled.

Water is off for part of each day. Plan a way to cook, wash and use the toilet. Some homeowners stay with family or use a rental during the heaviest days.

Local Note

Lehi Homes, Lead And Permits

A valve swap on older plumbing is a good time to ask about lead, since pre 1986 homes are the ones the city flags. The culinary system had its own lead free piping project in 1989. If your house is older, ask the city about lead testing before you plan the work.

The city building office issues permits and books inspections, so ask it about a whole house replacement early. Ask whether a replacement needs a permit before you sign a contract.

Choosing A Contractor

Questions To Ask Before You Hire

Ask every bidder

  • Which Utah license category do you hold and can I look it up?
  • Do you carry insurance for this work?
  • What is the written scope and the price before work starts?
  • How will you handle wall patching?
  • What inspections will you schedule?

The Utah Division of Professional Licensing runs a license lookup and can be reached at (801) 530-6628. We welcome these questions on our own jobs.

Preparation

What To Do Before The Crew Arrives

Clear access to walls, closets and the area around the water heater. Move furniture away from bathroom and kitchen walls. Take photos of the rooms before work starts so you can compare later.

Replacement quotes are available in Lehi and American Fork. Browse the service areas page for other towns, or our leak detection service if you want to find out whether a leak is the real problem before committing to replacement.

Planning

Preparing Your Household For The Work

A whole house replacement means water off for part of each day and open access in walls, ceilings or crawl spaces. Ask for the schedule in plain terms, including which days the kitchen and bathrooms will be unusable. Plan meals, laundry and showers around it.

Move furniture and valuables away from the walls where crews will cut access holes. Keep pets and small children in a separate area because of tools and open holes. Tell the crew about plaster, textured finishes or older drywall that is hard to patch.

Ask who handles patching and paint. Some plumbers leave rough openings for a drywall contractor and some include basic patching. Get the answer in writing with the price before work starts.

Hiring

Questions To Ask A Contractor

Ask which pipe material will be used and why, how many access holes to expect and who handles permit questions. Ask how long the water will be off each day and what happens if the crew finds a surprise behind a wall. Written answers help you compare quotes.
