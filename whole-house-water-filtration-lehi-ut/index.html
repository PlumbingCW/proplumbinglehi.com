Whole House Water Filtration In Lehi: What To Know First

A whole house filter treats water where it enters the home. Test first, pick the filter that fits your problem and plan the install.

By the Lehi Pro Plumbing team · Published November 3, 2026

Talk to a plumber

A whole house water filtration system treats water at the point where it enters your home, so every tap gets filtered water. Which filter you need depends on what is in your water, and the only way to know is to test. Common filters handle sediment, chlorine taste and some chemicals, while softeners and reverse osmosis handle other jobs.

This guide explains the main systems and what to test first. If you want help sizing equipment, our softener and filtration page covers testing and installation.

Quick Guide

Match The Filter To The Problem

What you noticeCommon filter typeWhat it does
Chlorine taste or smellActivated carbonReduces chlorine and some taste and odor compounds
Cloudy water or particlesSediment filterTraps sand, rust flakes and debris
White scale and spotted glassesWater softenerRemoves hardness minerals
Taste at the kitchen tap onlyReverse osmosis at the sinkReduces many dissolved solids
Concern about leadFilter certified for lead reductionReduces lead if certified and maintained

No single filter does everything. A combination of a sediment filter, a carbon filter and a softener is common, and an RO tap is often added at the kitchen sink.

Know Your Water

Test Before You Buy

Home test kits and lab tests tell you hardness, chlorine and other values. The result decides which filter makes sense. Buying a system before you test can lead to equipment that does not fit the problem.

What to test for

  • Hardness, if you see scale
  • Chlorine, if taste is the complaint
  • Iron and sediment, if you see stains or cloudiness
  • Lead, if your home is older
  • Total dissolved solids, if you are considering RO
Local Facts

What Lehi City Reports

Published city water quality reports list sodium, sulfate and total dissolved solids ranges and name chlorine as the disinfectant. The reports we reviewed do not publish a hardness number, so we cannot give one for your address.

The city reports its lead and copper sampling and says homes built before 1986 are more likely to have lead pipes or solder in house plumbing. If you are concerned, ask the city about testing and consider a filter certified for lead reduction as a stopgap while you plan a repipe.

Local Note

Irrigation Water Is Separate

Lehi has a separate pressurized irrigation system that carries untreated water to yards. The water that runs through your indoor taps is the culinary system. A whole house filter belongs on the culinary line only.

If your hose bib or outdoor line is connected to irrigation water, do not use it for drinking, filling pools or washing food.

Planning

Cost Factors

System type, size and installation difficulty drive price. Larger households need higher flow filters, and a softener needs a drain connection and sometimes an outlet. We give a clear price before work starts after we see your test result.

Water testing and equipment sizing happen in Lehi and in American Fork. The service area page has the full coverage list, or read about tankless water heaters if scale is hurting a heater.

Upkeep

Maintenance You Should Plan For

Every filter needs new media or new cartridges on a schedule. Sediment filters clog first, and low pressure at the tap is often the first clue. Ask for the maker replacement interval and set a reminder on your phone.

Carbon filters lose their ability to reduce taste and odor as they fill. A filter kept in service too long stops doing its job. Keep a spare cartridge on hand and write the install date on the housing.

A bypass valve and pressure gauges around the filter let you service it without shutting off the whole house. Ask us to include those parts in the quote.

Buying Smart

Questions To Ask Before You Sign

Ask what complaint or contaminant the system is meant to address and how the maker backs that claim. Look for independent certification for the specific claim, such as lead reduction, rather than a general marketing line. A system matched to your own test result is worth more than one with a long feature list.

Ask for the full cost over several years, including cartridges, salt or media and any service visits. Ask where the unit will sit, how much clearance it needs and whether it needs a drain or an outlet nearby.
